Harness gains come from tools and memory, not from the system prompt

  • Source: arXiv:2604.25850 v3, April 30, 2026 — cs.SE
  • Link: Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses — arXiv
  • TL;DR: Researchers built a system that watches execution traces and iteratively refines coding-agent harnesses. After 10 automated improvement rounds, task-completion rates climbed measurably. Controlled ablations traced every gain to changes in tool definitions, middleware (error handling, output formatting), and long-term memory management — not to any rewrites of the system prompt. The improved harness transferred intact to a different benchmark and three different base models; the system prompt's strategic framing did not transfer.
  • What to do: When you hit a plateau on agent task completion, audit and iterate on your tool schemas, tool-call error handling, and memory management before rewriting the system prompt.
  • Why trust it: Controlled ablations on Terminal-Bench 2 with cross-benchmark transfer to SWE-bench Verified and cross-model-family tests on three alternate base models; pass@1 lifted from 69.7% to 77.0% over 10 iterations, outperforming a human-designed harness baseline (71.9%) and two self-evolving baselines. Submitted to arXiv April 28, last revised April 30, 2026.
  • Skeptic check: Terminal-Bench 2 focuses on terminal-based coding tasks; findings may not generalize to harnesses built for browser use, document Q&A, or retrieval-heavy workflows where memory architecture and context management differ substantially.
